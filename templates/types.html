Types of Diabetes

Understanding the different types of diabetes and how they affect the body

Type 1 Diabetes

An autoimmune condition where the body’s immune system attacks insulin-producing cells in the pancreas.

Key Characteristics

  • Usually diagnosed in children, teens, and young adults
  • Body produces little or no insulin
  • Requires daily insulin therapy
  • Accounts for 5–10% of diabetes cases
  • Symptoms can develop quickly over weeks

Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause is unknown, but genetics and environmental factors like viruses may play a role.

Management

Daily insulin injections or insulin pump, blood sugar monitoring, healthy diet, and regular physical activity.

Type 2 Diabetes

A metabolic disorder where the body doesn’t use insulin properly (insulin resistance).

Key Characteristics

  • Most common form of diabetes (90–95% of cases)
  • Usually develops in adults over 45
  • Increasingly diagnosed in children and teens
  • Often preventable or can be delayed
  • May not show symptoms for years

Causes & Risk Factors

Lifestyle factors (obesity, physical inactivity), family history, age, and ethnicity are risk factors.

Management

Healthy eating, regular exercise, weight management, oral medications or insulin if needed, and blood sugar monitoring.

Gestational Diabetes

Diabetes that develops during pregnancy in women who didn’t previously have diabetes.

Key Characteristics

  • Develops during the second or third trimester
  • Affects 2–10% of pregnancies in the US
  • Usually goes away after delivery
  • Increases risk of Type 2 diabetes later
  • Can affect baby’s health if not managed

Causes & Risk Factors

Hormones from the placenta can block insulin’s action. Extra weight before pregnancy may also play a role.

Management

Blood sugar monitoring, healthy eating, physical activity, and sometimes insulin or medication.

Important Note

While these are the main types of diabetes, there are also other less common forms such as monogenic diabetes and diabetes caused by other conditions. If you're experiencing symptoms or have concerns about diabetes, it's essential to consult with a healthcare professional for proper diagnosis and treatment.
